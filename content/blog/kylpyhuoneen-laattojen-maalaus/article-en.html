Ceramic tiles in good condition can be painted with a purpose-made product when the surface is clean, dry and sound. Paint changes the colour and finish while leaving the tile layout and grout lines visible. A durable result depends above all on careful cleaning, the correct primer and sufficient curing time.

In brief: bathroom wall tiles can often be painted when the structure and waterproofing are in good condition. OIKOS Ecosmalto per Ceramica is not recommended for shower floors, surfaces with standing water or floors exposed to heavy traffic.

When does painting tiles make sense?

Tile painting is a practical option when the existing surface is technically sound and the aim is to update the colour or atmosphere of the room. It is lighter work than removing tiles, but a lasting finish requires an honest assessment of the substrate.

  • The tiles are firmly attached and free from cracks or chips.
  • The grout is intact, dry and clean.
  • There are no signs of moisture damage, mould or leakage.
  • The goal is a visual update, not concealing a structural fault.
  • The surface is not exposed to standing water or very heavy wear.

Tile paint and waterproofing are different things

In a wet room, surface treatment does not replace waterproofing. Under Finland's Ministry of the Environment Decree 782/2017, the structure behind surfaces exposed to running water, repeated splash water or condensing water must be waterproofed. A coat of paint renews the visible surface; it cannot repair old, damaged or missing waterproofing.

Stop work and investigate the structure if you find loose tiles, extensive cracks, grout that repeatedly turns dark, an unusual odour, softened structures, mould or a suspected leak.

How to paint tiles step by step

1. Protect the room

Remove cover plates, hooks and small fittings where possible. Protect furniture, floors and adjoining surfaces. Remove old silicone where it will be renewed, because paint does not adhere reliably to silicone.

2. Clean the tiles and grout thoroughly

Grease, soap residue, limescale, dust and detergent film all weaken adhesion. Clean the tiles, grout, corners, pipe surrounds and wall-to-floor junctions with a suitable product. Rinse carefully and allow the entire substrate to dry completely.

{{IMG_CLEANING}}

Removing grease, limescale and detergent residue is decisive for adhesion. Illustration: Karola G / Pexels.

3. Repair damaged grout

Repair loose or crumbling grout with a suitable material and observe its drying time. Paint cannot strengthen a weak substrate or stop movement in the structure.

4. Dull a very smooth or glossy surface

Light sanding removes gloss and improves mechanical adhesion. Use fine sandpaper or a sanding pad, avoid aggressive scratching and remove all sanding dust.

5. Prime according to the substrate

Use Aggrappante Ecologico on very smooth, glossy or otherwise difficult surfaces. According to the technical data, apply one even coat; it can be overcoated after about six hours, depending on conditions.

6. Apply two thin coats

Stir the paint thoroughly. Brush the grout, corners and difficult areas first, then apply the paint evenly with a short-nap roller. Two thin coats produce a more controlled finish than one thick coat. OIKOS specifies 6–8 hours between coats.

7. Allow the surface to cure

A touch-dry surface is not yet fully durable. Ecosmalto per Ceramica reaches maximum strength after eight days, and washing should be avoided for at least seven days.

How much tile paint is needed?

The stated coverage is approximately 12–14 m²/l per coat. Two coats are required. Add about ten per cent for practical allowance:

surface area × 2 coats ÷ coverage × 1.10

Common mistakes

  • Painting before the grout has dried completely.
  • Leaving grease, limescale or silicone residue on the surface.
  • Failing to dull or prime a glossy substrate when required.
  • Applying the paint too thickly, causing runs in the grout lines.
  • Putting the surface into service before it has cured.
  • Using paint to conceal a moisture problem.
  • Painting an area where water can remain standing.

Caring for painted tiles

  • Avoid washing for at least seven days.
  • Use a mild detergent and a soft cloth or sponge.
  • Avoid steel wool, strongly abrasive powders and aggressive chemicals.
  • Remove splashes regularly and repair local damage promptly in accordance with the product instructions.
